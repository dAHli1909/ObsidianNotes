# ActionPotentials (the fire of a neuron)

## Computer-like
They are a series of ONs and OFFs that the neurons use to create a sort of code as complex as with computers; it either happens or simply doesn't. Actually, there are graded potentials, which mostly don't really affect in any way the calculations a region may create. 
It either happens or it doesn't; the only thing that can vary is the frequency in which the neuron fires.

### Dendrites:

## How the membrane works

![[Pasted image 20240528165545.png]]

**"When a neuron isn't firing, it is considered to be at rest"**

In a typical neuron in a resting state, Na+ is more present on the outside
This generates a chemical gradient, which thanks to the fact that we're treating with charged particles, also creates an electrical gradient (as no particle of different element composition has the same charge, in spite of it being positive or negative)

 There is a generally more positive charge on the outside of the cell. 

![[Pasted image 20240528165751.png]]


"The difference in total charge inside and outside of the cell is called the membrane potential. "
Resting potential: -70 milivolts ("The inside of the cell is approximately 70 mV less positive than the outside")
